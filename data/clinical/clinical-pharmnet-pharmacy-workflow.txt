# Cerner PharmNet Pharmacy Workflow Guide

## What is PharmNet?

PharmNet is Cerner Millennium's integrated pharmacy module. It provides a complete pharmacy information system covering inpatient and outpatient pharmacy operations: medication order verification, dispensing workflow, medication preparation (IV compounding), clinical pharmacist review, controlled substance tracking, and formulary management.

PharmNet integrates tightly with PowerChart CPOE (orders originate in CPOE), eMAR (administration records visible to pharmacy), and BCMA (dispensing confirmation for barcode administration).

## PharmNet Core Modules

- **Inpatient Pharmacy** — handles acute care medication verification, dispensing, and ADC (Automated Dispensing Cabinet) management
- **Outpatient Pharmacy** — handles retail/ambulatory prescription dispensing, pricing, and label printing
- **IV Room / IV Flow** — manages IV admixture compounding workflows with gravimetric or barcode verification
- **Controlled Substance Management** — tracks controlled substance dispensing, returns, and waste reconciliation
- **Pharmacist Clinical Review** — tools for clinical pharmacist activities: medication reconciliation, anticoagulation management, renal dose checking, kinetics dosing

## Inpatient Pharmacy Workflow

### Order Verification Queue

When a provider places a medication order in CPOE:
1. The order appears immediately in the PharmNet verification queue.
2. Orders are prioritized by STAT flag and urgency.
3. The verifying pharmacist sees: medication name, dose, route, frequency, ordering provider, allergy profile, and current medication list.

Pharmacist verification steps:
1. Open the order in the verification queue.
2. Review: clinical appropriateness, dose (may use weight-based dosing calculator), route, frequency, allergy cross-references, drug-drug interactions.
3. If appropriate: click Verify — order status changes to "Verified" and the medication activates in eMAR.
4. If modification needed: edit dose/route/frequency and document the change reason; re-route to ordering provider if required.
5. If inappropriate: click Deny or Contact Provider — order returns to the provider with a pharmacist note.

### Dispensing Queue

After verification:
1. The medication appears in the dispensing queue with the patient's location and assigned ADC.
2. Pharmacy tech retrieves or prepares the medication.
3. For unit-dose oral medications: retrieve from bulk stock or repackager; apply patient-specific barcode label if not pre-labeled.
4. For IV medications: see IV Room workflow.
5. Pharmacist performs final check (verify product matches order).
6. Dispense event recorded: medication, dose, ADC/location, dispense time.
7. Medication delivered to unit ADC or directly to nurse.

### ADC (Automated Dispensing Cabinet) Integration

Pyxis, Omnicell, and other ADC vendors integrate with PharmNet:
- PharmNet pushes verified, dispensed medication profiles to the ADC.
- The ADC only unlocks the correct medication pocket for verified medications.
- Override functionality allows emergency access with documented reason.
- ADC transactions flow back to PharmNet for charge capture and controlled substance reconciliation.

## IV Room Workflow

### IV Admixture Preparation

1. Pharmacist verifies the IV order in the verification queue.
2. A preparation label is generated for the IV room.
3. Pharmacy tech prepares the IV admixture:
   - Retrieves base solution (NS, D5W, LR) and drug additive vials.
   - Scans base solution barcode and drug additive barcode (gravimetric or barcode verification).
   - Mixes the IV solution using aseptic technique.
4. Pharmacist performs final IV check (correct drug, concentration, volume, patient label, expiration).
5. IV bag labeled with: patient name, MRN, medication name, concentration, rate, infusion instructions, and expiration date/time.
6. IV bag delivered to the nursing unit.

### IV Barcode Verification (IVFlow)

PharmNet IVFlow adds barcode scanning verification to IV preparation:
- Scan each additive barcode during compounding.
- System confirms each scanned item matches the compounding order.
- Reduces preparation errors from look-alike/sound-alike vials.
- Gravimetric verification (weighing before and after additive injection) provides a secondary check.

## Controlled Substance Management

### Tracking Controlled Substances

All Schedule II–V controlled substances are tracked from receipt to administration or waste:
1. **Receipt**: Pharmacy receives controlled substances from wholesaler. DEA order form (222) reconciled with received quantity. Recorded in PharmNet inventory.
2. **Dispensing**: Controlled substances dispensed to ADC or directly to nurse. Each dispense decrements perpetual inventory.
3. **Administration**: Nurse removes ordered dose from ADC. BCMA confirms administration.
4. **Waste**: Unused partial doses must be witnessed and wasted. Waste recorded in ADC and PharmNet.
5. **Returns**: Unopened medications returned from the unit reconciled back to PharmNet inventory.

### Controlled Substance Reconciliation

Discrepancy resolution:
- Run the Controlled Substance Reconciliation report in PharmNet daily.
- Any count discrepancy (dispensed ≠ administered + wasted + returned) triggers an investigation.
- Unresolved discrepancies must be reported to the pharmacist in charge and potentially to the DEA.

## Outpatient / Retail Pharmacy Workflow

For organizations with an outpatient pharmacy integrated with Cerner:
1. Provider generates a prescription from CPOE or from the ambulatory visit.
2. Prescription transmits electronically (via SureScripts or direct integration) to the PharmNet outpatient queue.
3. Pharmacy technician enters or reviews the prescription.
4. Pharmacist verifies: drug, dose, route, frequency, days' supply, allergy check, insurance prior authorization status.
5. Technician fills the prescription.
6. Pharmacist performs final dispensing check.
7. Patient counseling offered at pickup.
8. Label printed with patient name, prescriber, medication, dose, route, frequency, refills remaining.

## Formulary Management

PharmNet manages the organization's drug formulary:
- **Formulary** — approved medications available for ordering in CPOE.
- **Non-formulary** — medications requiring special approval (requires pharmacist or P&T committee authorization).
- **Therapeutic substitution** — automatic substitution of a formulary equivalent for a non-formulary order (must be approved by the P&T committee and disclosed in the order).
- **Dosing limits** — maximum doses enforced during verification; alerts display if an ordered dose exceeds the limit.

Formulary management tasks:
1. Open PharmNet Administration > Formulary Management.
2. Add/remove medications, set default doses and dosing limits.
3. Configure therapeutic substitution rules.
4. Update drug pricing and NDC records.

## Clinical Pharmacist Tools

### Anticoagulation Management
PharmNet provides a dedicated anticoagulation module:
- Heparin drip protocol management with real-time aPTT dose adjustment.
- Warfarin dosing calculator with INR trend tracking.
- Direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) monitoring.
- Integration with lab results for automatic dose recommendations.

### Renal Dose Adjustment
- Pharmacist receives alerts for renally-cleared medications when patient's creatinine clearance falls below thresholds.
- PharmNet calculates CrCl from the most recent serum creatinine and patient demographics.
- Dose adjustment recommendations based on configured protocols.

### Kinetics Dosing (Vancomycin / Aminoglycosides)
- Pharmacokinetics dosing module calculates individualized dosing for vancomycin and aminoglycosides based on levels drawn at specific times.
- Integrated with lab results pull from CLINICAL_EVENT.

## Reporting and Analytics

Key PharmNet reports available via Discern Analytics:
- Medication override report by unit and by drug
- Controlled substance reconciliation summary
- Formulary compliance rate
- Antibiotic stewardship reports (days of therapy, defined daily dose)
- ADC inventory variance report
- Time to first dose (for STAT medications)
- Pharmacy verification turnaround time

## Best Practices

1. Prioritize STAT orders — ensure verification within 15–30 minutes of order placement.
2. Never skip the final dispensing check — the pharmacist check is the last safety barrier before the patient receives the medication.
3. Investigate every ADC override — high override rates indicate formulary gaps or workflow issues.
4. Reconcile controlled substances daily — do not allow discrepancies to accumulate.
5. Document every drug-drug interaction override with clinical rationale.
6. Keep the formulary current — outdated entries cause CPOE ordering confusion.
