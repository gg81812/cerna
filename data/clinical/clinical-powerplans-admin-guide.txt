# Cerner PowerPlans (Order Sets) Administrator Guide

## What are PowerPlans?

PowerPlans (also known as Order Sets in some Cerner documentation) are pre-configured, evidence-based collections of orders that providers initiate with a single action in PowerChart. They standardize care pathways for common clinical scenarios — such as admission for pneumonia, hip fracture post-operative care, or sepsis management — by presenting a structured set of recommended orders that the provider can review and modify before signing.

PowerPlans reduce variation in care, improve adherence to evidence-based guidelines, and reduce time spent on repetitive order entry.

## Types of PowerPlans

### Admission PowerPlans
Comprehensive order sets used at the time of inpatient admission for a specific diagnosis or procedure. Typically include: diet, activity level, vital sign frequency, nursing tasks, IV access, medications, lab orders, and imaging orders.

Example: "Pneumonia Admission — Community Acquired" includes:
- Activity: Out of bed with assistance
- Diet: Regular as tolerated
- Vital signs: q4h
- Pulse oximetry: continuous
- IV access: Peripheral IV × 1
- IV fluids: Normal Saline 0.9% at 125 mL/hr
- Medications: Azithromycin 500mg PO daily, Ceftriaxone 1g IV q24h
- Labs: CBC, CMP, Procalcitonin, Blood cultures × 2
- Imaging: Chest X-ray PA/Lateral

### Procedure / Post-Op PowerPlans
Used after specific surgical or procedural interventions. Include post-procedure monitoring orders, activity restrictions, wound care, pain management, and DVT prophylaxis.

### Protocol-Based PowerPlans
Used for clinical protocols requiring frequent adjustments. Examples:
- Sepsis Bundle (Surviving Sepsis Campaign)
- Insulin Infusion Protocol
- Heparin Drip Protocol
- Alcohol Withdrawal Protocol (CIWA)

These often include conditional order logic (if/then branching) based on clinical parameters.

### Phase-Based PowerPlans
Complex plans divided into phases that activate at different times in the care episode:
- Phase 1: Operative
- Phase 2: PACU (Recovery)
- Phase 3: Floor
- Phase 4: Discharge

Orders in each phase activate when the phase is initiated by nursing or provider.

## Building a PowerPlan in PowerChart Administration

### Accessing PowerPlan Administration
1. Open PowerChart Administration.
2. Navigate to Clinical Administration > Order Management > PowerPlan Administration.
3. The console shows existing PowerPlans organized by type and specialty.

### Creating a New PowerPlan

1. Click "New PowerPlan."
2. Enter a name (e.g., "Hip Arthroplasty Post-Op — Adult"), description, and owning specialty.
3. Select PowerPlan type (Admission, Procedure, Protocol, Phase-Based).
4. Set status to "In Development."

### Adding Order Sections

Organize orders into logical sections:
- Section examples: Activity, Diet, Vital Signs, Monitoring, IV Access, Medications, Labs, Imaging, Nursing Orders.
- Each section has a section name and display order (sequence in which sections appear to the provider).

### Adding Individual Orders

Within each section, add orders:
1. Click "Add Order" in the section.
2. Search for the order from the CPOE order catalog by name, synonym, or order code.
3. Select the order.
4. Configure order details: dose, route, frequency, duration, start time offset (e.g., "Start 2 hours after plan initiation"), and any required additional order details.
5. Set order status:
   - **Pre-selected (checked)** — order appears checked by default; provider must uncheck to exclude.
   - **Unchecked (optional)** — order appears unchecked; provider must check to include.
   - **Required** — order cannot be removed by the provider.

### Conditional/Branching Orders

For protocol-based plans with conditional logic:
1. Add an "IF/THEN" conditional group.
2. Define the condition (e.g., "If patient weight > 100 kg").
3. Add one set of orders for the Yes branch and another for the No branch.
4. The provider sees only the applicable branch based on patient data.

### Medications in PowerPlans

Medication orders in PowerPlans can be configured with:
- Default dose with allowed range (e.g., "Acetaminophen 650mg PO q6h PRN pain — dose range 325–1000mg")
- Weight-based dosing (dose calculated automatically from patient weight in Millennium)
- Allergy-linked alternatives (if patient allergic to penicillin, display alternative antibiotic)
- Duration and stop date/time

## PowerPlan Governance

### Physician/Pharmacist Review

All PowerPlans must be reviewed and approved before activation:
1. Pharmacy reviews all medication orders for formulary compliance and dosing.
2. Medical staff committee (or specialty committee) reviews for clinical appropriateness and evidence base.
3. Nursing reviews nursing orders and tasks.
4. Compliance reviews for regulatory requirements.

Formal approval is documented in the organization's PowerPlan governance log.

### Evidence Base Documentation
Each PowerPlan should have an associated evidence summary document (attached in the PowerPlan metadata field) citing:
- Clinical guidelines (e.g., Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2023)
- Hospital policy references
- Specialty society recommendations

### Annual Review Cycle
PowerPlans should be reviewed annually (or whenever referenced guidelines are updated) by the owning clinical committee to ensure continued accuracy and evidence alignment.

## Activating and Deploying PowerPlans

### Activation
1. After governance approval, open the PowerPlan in Administration.
2. Set status to "Active."
3. Assign the PowerPlan to appropriate facilities and departments in the Assignment configuration.
4. Set the PowerPlan catalog entry (how it appears in the provider's search).

### Access Control
Control which providers can initiate which PowerPlans:
- PowerPlan can be restricted to specific positions (e.g., Orthopedic Surgery only).
- Or made available to all ordering providers.

## Using PowerPlans in Clinical Workflow

### Provider Initiates a PowerPlan

1. Provider opens the patient's chart in PowerChart.
2. Navigates to the Orders section.
3. Selects "Add PowerPlan" or searches the PowerPlan catalog.
4. Selects the appropriate PowerPlan.
5. The plan opens with all pre-selected orders checked.
6. Provider reviews each section:
   - Unchecks any orders not appropriate for this patient.
   - Checks any optional orders that are needed.
   - Modifies doses, routes, frequencies as clinically appropriate.
7. Provider signs — all checked orders route to pharmacy, nursing, radiology, and lab per their respective workflows.

### Modifying an Active PowerPlan
After signing, the provider can:
- Add orders: initiate additional individual orders alongside the plan.
- Modify individual orders: open the order and change details.
- Discontinue orders: right-click and discontinue.
- Transition to next phase (for phase-based plans).

## Best Practices for PowerPlan Build

1. Keep PowerPlans focused — one condition, one patient population per plan. Broad "catch-all" plans confuse providers.
2. Pre-select evidence-based defaults — providers can opt out, but defaults should reflect best practice.
3. Avoid very long plans — plans with >50 orders have low completion rates. Break into specialty sub-plans.
4. Use consistent naming conventions — makes searching the catalog easier.
5. Include pharmacy in the review process for every medication order.
6. Date-stamp and version-track all changes — use the governance log.
7. Audit plan utilization via Discern Analytics — low utilization may indicate a usability or workflow fit problem.
8. Test in CERT with a simulated patient before activating in Production.
