# Cerner PowerChart Clinical Decision Support (CDS) Guide

## What is Clinical Decision Support?

Clinical Decision Support (CDS) in Cerner PowerChart refers to a set of automated tools that provide clinicians with relevant, actionable information and alerts at the point of care to improve decision-making. CDS encompasses: order entry alerts (drug interactions, allergy checks, dose range warnings), best practice advisories (BPAs), clinical reminders, care gap notifications, and quality measure dashboards.

Cerner's CDS framework integrates with CPOE, documentation workflows, and the clinical data repository to fire rules based on real-time patient data.

## CDS Alert Categories

### Order Entry CDS

Fires during CPOE order entry:

**Allergy Alerts**
- Triggered when an ordered medication matches a documented patient allergy or has a cross-reactive compound match.
- Alert levels: Hard stop (order cannot proceed without override) or Soft stop (override allowed with reason).
- Hard stops are typically reserved for anaphylaxis-documented allergies to the same drug class.

**Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) Alerts**
- Triggered when the new medication interacts with an existing active medication.
- Severity tiers: Contraindicated (highest), Severe, Moderate, Mild.
- Contraindicated alerts should function as hard stops.
- Moderate and Mild alerts are typically soft stops — provider must acknowledge.
- Alert source: First DataBank (FDB) or Multum drug interaction database, integrated with Millennium.

**Drug-Disease Interaction Alerts**
- Triggered when an ordered medication has a known risk in the context of a patient's documented diagnosis.
- Example: NSAID ordered for a patient with documented CKD Stage 3.

**Dose Range Alerts**
- Triggered when the ordered dose falls outside the expected therapeutic range.
- Weight-based dose range checks for pediatrics and oncology.
- Uses patient weight from the most recent vital signs.

**Duplicate Order Alerts**
- Triggered when a similar order is already active.
- Prevents double-ordering of the same medication or lab test.

**Formulary Alerts**
- Triggered when a non-formulary medication is ordered.
- Suggests therapeutic substitution alternatives.

### Best Practice Advisories (BPAs)

BPAs are proactive, evidence-based reminders that appear in the provider's workflow — typically during chart review or order entry — when patient data triggers a clinical recommendation.

Examples:
- "Patient has not received an influenza vaccine this season — order now?"
- "Patient meets sepsis criteria (2 SIRS criteria + suspected infection) — consider Sepsis Bundle initiation."
- "Patient with diabetes — last HbA1c > 3 months ago — order now?"
- "Patient on warfarin — INR not checked in 7 days — order INR."
- "Patient on opioids for >7 days — consider naloxone co-prescription per hospital policy."

BPAs are configured by clinical informatics with input from quality, pharmacy, and medical staff. Each BPA has:
- Trigger condition (logic evaluated against patient data)
- Recommendation text
- One-click action (order, document, or navigate to relevant screen)
- Override reason options
- Acknowledgment requirement

### Clinical Reminders

Similar to BPAs but focused on periodic preventive care tasks and chronic disease management gaps:
- Colorectal cancer screening due
- Mammogram overdue
- Blood pressure out of target range (chronic hypertension management)
- BMI screening and counseling
- Annual depression screening

Clinical reminders appear in the provider's InBasket or on the patient summary MPage.

### Inpatient-Specific CDS

**Deterioration Index / Early Warning Scores**
- Cerner's Patient Deterioration Detection (PDD) or the Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS) calculates risk of clinical deterioration based on vital signs, lab values, and nursing assessments.
- Alerts escalate through nursing, charge nurse, and rapid response team tiers based on score thresholds.
- Alerts appear in the nursing Workflow MPage and can generate InBasket messages to the attending provider.

**Sepsis Alert**
- Automatically identifies patients meeting Sepsis-3 criteria (SOFA score or clinical suspicion + organ dysfunction).
- Generates a BPA to the ordering provider with a one-click sepsis bundle initiation.
- Tracks bundle compliance for quality reporting.

**VTE Prophylaxis Reminder**
- Identifies inpatients without active DVT prophylaxis (LMWH or sequential compression device orders).
- Generates a reminder to order VTE prophylaxis or document clinical contraindication.

**Antibiotic Stewardship Alerts**
- Identifies patients on broad-spectrum antibiotics for > 72 hours without culture results.
- Prompts de-escalation consideration.
- Alerts patients with positive culture results — de-escalate to targeted therapy.

## Configuring CDS Alerts

### Accessing CDS Administration

1. Log in as an administrator with Clinical Decision Support access.
2. Navigate to PowerChart Administration > CDS > Rule Administration.
3. The CDS console shows all active and inactive rules.

### Configuring BPA Logic

Each BPA rule is built with:
- **Trigger Event** — what action fires the rule evaluation (order entry, chart open, documentation sign).
- **Patient Criteria** — CCL-like expressions evaluating patient data (age, diagnoses, lab values, active orders, vitals).
- **Recommendation Text** — the message displayed to the provider.
- **Action Buttons** — one-click actions (Add to Orders, Document, Dismiss, Defer).
- **Override Reasons** — code value list of acceptable reasons to dismiss the alert without acting.
- **Firing Frequency** — how often the same alert re-fires for the same patient (once per encounter, daily, on each login, etc.).

### Alert Fatigue Management

Alert fatigue — where clinicians override alerts reflexively without reading them — is a major patient safety risk. Mitigation strategies:

1. **Reduce low-value alerts** — quarterly review of override rates; deactivate alerts with >95% override rates without action.
2. **Tier alert severity** — not every alert needs to be a hard stop. Use hard stops sparingly (life-threatening situations only).
3. **Contextualize alerts** — show the patient-specific data driving the alert (e.g., "Renal function: CrCl 18 mL/min — dose adjustment recommended").
4. **Simplify override reasons** — long lists of reasons make overrides feel laborious; keep to 3–5 meaningful choices.
5. **Measure, monitor, and optimize** — track alert fire rates, override rates, and workflow impact monthly.

### CDS Alert Performance Metrics

Key metrics tracked via Discern Analytics:
- Alert fire rate per 100 orders
- Alert acceptance rate (% acted upon vs. overridden)
- Override rate by alert type and by provider
- Time to acknowledge alerts
- Impact on order workflow (orders placed after alert vs. cancelled)

## Drug Database Integration

Cerner integrates with clinical drug knowledge databases (First DataBank or Multum) that are updated quarterly:
- Drug-drug interaction tables
- Allergy cross-reactivity tables
- Dose range reference data
- Drug-disease contraindication data

After each database update, clinical pharmacy reviews changes for impact and adjusts alert configurations as needed.

## Best Practices

1. Calibrate alert thresholds carefully — a well-calibrated CDS system fires actionable alerts at a rate of <5 per encounter.
2. Review alert override rates monthly — alerts overridden >90% of the time are causing fatigue without delivering value.
3. Involve frontline clinicians in BPA design — alerts built without provider input have low adoption and high override rates.
4. Document the evidence base for every BPA — alerts without cited evidence lose credibility quickly.
5. Test CDS rules in CERT before activating in Production — logic errors in live CDS rules cause patient safety events.
6. Periodically sunset outdated alerts — guidelines change; old CDS rules can become inaccurate or harmful over time.
