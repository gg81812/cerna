# Cerner eMAR (Electronic Medication Administration Record) User Guide

## What is the eMAR?

The Electronic Medication Administration Record (eMAR) in Cerner Millennium is the digital replacement for the paper Medication Administration Record (MAR). It displays all active medication orders for a patient, tracks scheduled and as-needed (PRN) administration times, and provides the interface through which nurses document medication administration events.

The eMAR is integrated with PharmNet (Cerner's pharmacy module) and BCMA (Barcode Medication Administration). Medications flow into the eMAR only after they are ordered in CPOE and verified by pharmacy.

## Accessing the eMAR

### From PowerChart
1. Open the patient's chart in PowerChart.
2. Click the eMAR tab in the chart menu (location varies by site configuration).
3. The eMAR opens in a dedicated window showing all current medication orders.

### Standalone eMAR Application
Many nursing units use the eMAR standalone application on workstations-on-wheels (WOWs):
1. Log in to the workstation with your AD credentials.
2. Open the eMAR icon on the desktop.
3. Search for the patient by name, MRN, or location/bed.

## eMAR Display Layout

### Medication List (Left Panel)
Lists all active medications grouped by:
- Schedule type: Continuous (scheduled q4h, q8h, daily, etc.) vs. PRN (as needed)
- Drug category: routine medications, IV medications, critical medications, etc. (grouping is site-configured)

Each medication row shows:
- Medication name and dose
- Route and frequency
- Pharmacy verification status (Verified, Pending, On Hold)
- STAT or HIGH_ALERT flags

### Administration Timeline (Right Panel)
A time-grid showing:
- Scheduled administration times for each medication
- Color-coded status bubbles:
  - Green: Administered on time
  - Yellow: Due within 30 minutes (or site-configured window)
  - Red: Overdue (past the administration window)
  - Gray: Given early or given late (within acceptable window but not on-time)
  - Blue: Not Given (reason documented)
  - White/Empty: Not yet due

### Administration Windows
By default, medications can be documented as "on time" within a 60-minute window around the scheduled time (30 min before to 30 min after). The administration window is configurable per site and per frequency.

## Documenting Medication Administration

### Standard Scheduled Medication

1. Click the due time bubble for the medication you are about to give.
2. If BCMA is enabled: scan the patient wristband, then scan the medication barcode.
3. The administration detail screen opens showing: medication name, dose, route, and scheduled time.
4. Verify the information is correct.
5. Click "Given" to document the administration.
6. System records: prsnl_id (nurse), timestamp, dose given, route, BCMA scan confirmation.

### PRN (As Needed) Medication

1. Click the PRN medication row.
2. Select "Give Now."
3. Complete BCMA scanning (if enabled).
4. Enter indication/reason for the PRN (required field — site-configurable code value list).
5. Click "Given."
6. PRN effectiveness follow-up may be prompted after a configured time interval (e.g., 30 min after a pain medication).

### IV Drip / Continuous Infusion

1. Click the continuous infusion medication row.
2. The IV administration screen shows: current rate, concentration, bag volume.
3. Document: rate change (if applicable), bag change, new bag number, volume infused.
4. Click "Save" to record the infusion documentation.

### "Not Given" Documentation

When a medication is not administered as scheduled:
1. Click the overdue time bubble.
2. Select "Not Given."
3. Enter a reason from the code value list:
   - Patient refused
   - Patient not on unit
   - Medication not available
   - Hold ordered
   - Clinical contraindication
   - Other (with free-text explanation)
4. Click "Save." The bubble turns blue in the timeline.

### Partial Dose or Dose Change

1. Click the medication bubble.
2. Select "Given" and modify the Dose Administered field before saving.
3. The partial dose is recorded with a note comparing ordered vs. given dose.

## Medication Status and Verification

### Pharmacy Verification States
- **Ordered** — order placed by provider but not yet reviewed by pharmacy.
- **Verified** — pharmacy has reviewed, adjusted if needed, and approved for administration. Nurses can only administer Verified medications.
- **On Hold** — pharmacy has placed the medication on hold (e.g., pending lab result, drug interaction review). Nurses must contact pharmacy before administering.
- **Discontinued** — medication removed from the eMAR; no further administration.

### STAT Medications
STAT orders appear in a dedicated STAT section at the top of the eMAR with a red STAT indicator. Pharmacy prioritizes STAT orders and typically verifies within 15–30 minutes. Nurses should monitor for the Verified status change before administering.

## PRN Effectiveness Assessments

For PRN medications configured with effectiveness follow-up (typically pain medications):
- A follow-up prompt appears in the eMAR at the configured interval after the PRN was given.
- The nurse documents the patient's response (e.g., pain score before and after).
- If a second dose is required before the allowed re-dose interval, the eMAR alerts the nurse.

## High-Alert Medication Workflow

Medications flagged as HIGH_ALERT (insulin, heparin, chemotherapy, narcotics) require:
1. Two-nurse BCMA verification (both nurses scan their badges or login credentials).
2. Both nurses review and confirm the medication, dose, and patient.
3. Both nurse IDs are recorded in the administration event.

## Filtering and Sorting the eMAR

- **Due Now filter** — shows only medications due within the current administration window.
- **All Medications** — shows entire eMAR including medications not yet due.
- **Critical / High Alert filter** — shows only flagged medications.
- **IV filter** — shows only infusions and IV medications.
- **Search by medication name** — filter the medication list by drug name.

## Common Workflows

### Shift Start Check
1. Filter by "Due Now" to see which medications need administration.
2. Review any red (overdue) bubbles and investigate or document "Not Given" with reason.
3. Check STAT orders and any new orders from the night shift.

### Medication Reconciliation at Discharge
1. Nursing completes eMAR administration documentation for all medications given.
2. Provider compares home medications to hospital medications in Medication Reconciliation (a separate module linked to the eMAR).
3. Discharge prescription generated from reconciled medication list.

## Printing the eMAR

1. Click the Print icon in the eMAR toolbar.
2. Select date range (typically current 24-hour period).
3. Select which medication categories to include.
4. Print or save as PDF for paper backup during downtime procedures.

## Troubleshooting

### Medication Not Appearing in eMAR
- Confirm the order is Active (not pending, discontinued, or on hold) in CPOE.
- Confirm pharmacy has verified the order (status must be Verified for it to appear as administerable).
- Check the eMAR filter — it may be filtered to a specific category that excludes the medication.

### Cannot Document Administration (Greyed Out)
- The medication may be On Hold — contact pharmacy.
- The administration window may have passed (medication is now more than 1 hour overdue).
- The user may lack the nursing administration security object — contact the help desk.

### BCMA Scan Failure
- See the BCMA Administrator Guide for detailed troubleshooting.
- If BCMA is temporarily unavailable, use the manual override with documented reason.

## Best Practices

1. Document administration at the time of administration — never document in advance or batch at end of shift.
2. Scan every time — do not skip BCMA scanning for convenience.
3. Always document "Not Given" with a reason rather than leaving bubbles red.
4. Check for new STAT orders before starting each medication pass.
5. Never administer a medication that shows "Ordered" (not Verified) status — contact pharmacy.
6. Document PRN effectiveness at the required follow-up interval.
