# Cerner PowerChart Medication Reconciliation Guide

## What is Medication Reconciliation?

Medication reconciliation is the clinical process of comparing a patient's current medication orders against all medications the patient has been taking at a prior care setting or at home. The goal is to identify and resolve discrepancies — omissions, duplications, dosing errors, or unsafe drug interactions — that arise during care transitions.

In Cerner Millennium, medication reconciliation is performed using the Medication Reconciliation module within PowerChart, which provides a side-by-side view of: Home Medications, Inpatient Orders, and the reconciled Discharge Medication List.

Medication reconciliation is required by The Joint Commission's National Patient Safety Goal (NPSG) 03.06.01 at every care transition: admission, transfer, and discharge.

## Medication Reconciliation in PowerChart

### Accessing Medication Reconciliation

From PowerChart:
1. Open the patient's chart.
2. Click "Medication Reconciliation" in the chart navigation, OR
3. Access from the discharge workflow prompt when a Discharge Order is placed.

### The Three-Column View

The Medication Reconciliation screen presents three columns:

**Column 1: Home Medications (Prior to Admission)**
Medications the patient was taking before the current encounter:
- Imported from the patient's medication history (from prior visits, ADT, or HIE connections)
- Manually entered by the admitting nurse or provider during the admission assessment
- Imported from the patient's MyChart self-reported medication list (if configured)
- Downloaded from external pharmacy records via SureScripts integration

Each home medication shows: drug name, dose, route, frequency, and source (patient-reported, pharmacy fill history, prior encounter).

**Column 2: Hospital / Inpatient Medications (Current Orders)**
The current active inpatient medication orders from CPOE:
- Linked in real time from the ORDERS table
- Shows: drug name, dose, route, frequency, order status (Ordered/Verified/Active)

**Column 3: Discharge Medications (Reconciled List)**
The medication list the patient will take after discharge:
- Built by the provider during medication reconciliation
- Each entry is one of: Continue (same drug/dose as home), Modify (dose or frequency changed), New Medication, Stop (discontinue at discharge)

### Completing Admission Medication Reconciliation

1. Open Medication Reconciliation at the time of admission.
2. Review Column 1 (Home Medications) with the patient or caregiver to confirm accuracy.
3. Add, edit, or remove medications from the home list as needed.
4. For each home medication, indicate what should happen during the hospital stay:
   - **Continue (reconcile to inpatient order)** — links the home medication to an active or new inpatient order.
   - **Hold** — medication held during hospitalization (document reason).
   - **No Inpatient Equivalent** — patient takes this medication at home but no inpatient order is needed.

The reconciliation confirms the inpatient orders represent an intentional, correct translation of the home medication list.

### Completing Discharge Medication Reconciliation

1. Open Medication Reconciliation when discharge is being planned.
2. Review all active inpatient orders (Column 2).
3. For each medication, specify what happens at discharge:
   - **Continue at home** — prescription sent to outpatient pharmacy; dose/route/frequency may be adjusted.
   - **Discontinue** — stop at discharge (document reason: completed course, side effect, no longer indicated).
   - **New medication** — started during hospitalization, to be continued at home.
4. Review Column 1 (home medications taken before admission):
   - Were all pre-admission medications either resumed or intentionally discontinued?
   - Are there any home medications that were not addressed during the reconciliation?
5. Generate the discharge prescription list from the reconciled Column 3.
6. Sign the Medication Reconciliation document — this creates a legal record of the reconciliation.

### Handling Discrepancies

When a discrepancy is found between the home medication list and the current inpatient orders:
1. The system flags medications that appear in Column 1 but have no corresponding inpatient order.
2. The provider reviews each flagged medication and either: places the missing inpatient order, OR documents "intentional omission" with a clinical reason.
3. Common discrepancies:
   - Home medication not continued without documentation
   - Dose changed without acknowledging the original home dose
   - Duplicate medications (e.g., two ACE inhibitors from different sources)

## Transfer Reconciliation

When a patient transfers between units (e.g., ICU to step-down unit):
1. The admitting provider for the receiving unit opens Medication Reconciliation.
2. Reviews orders active in the transferring unit.
3. Confirms which orders should continue in the new care setting.
4. Modifies or discontinues orders as appropriate for the new level of care.

Transfer reconciliation is often abbreviated compared to admission/discharge reconciliation but must address any care-setting-specific medications (e.g., insulin drips discontinued when ICU-level monitoring is no longer available).

## SureScripts Integration (Pharmacy Fill History)

When SureScripts is configured:
- Medication fill history from retail pharmacies is imported into the Home Medications list automatically.
- Provides a more objective medication history than patient self-report.
- Data availability depends on pharmacy participation (most large retail chains participate).
- Updates may lag 24–48 hours behind actual fills.

## Medication History Source Documentation

The Medication Reconciliation module tracks the source of each home medication entry:
- Patient/family report
- Pharmacy fill history (SureScripts)
- Prior encounter
- HIE (Health Information Exchange)
- Medication administration record from prior facility

Source documentation is important for identifying potentially unreliable entries (patient-reported medications have higher error rates than pharmacy records).

## Compliance and Reporting

Medication reconciliation compliance is tracked via Discern Analytics:
- % of admissions with completed medication reconciliation within 24 hours
- % of discharges with completed discharge medication reconciliation before discharge order close
- Discrepancy rates by unit, provider, or medication type
- Intentional omission documentation rates

Joint Commission surveys routinely review medication reconciliation documentation and audit for compliance.

## Best Practices

1. Perform admission medication reconciliation with the patient and/or a knowledgeable caregiver present — patient-reported lists alone are unreliable.
2. Use SureScripts pharmacy fill data as a cross-reference, not as the sole source — patients may take medications not filled at a pharmacy.
3. Document every intentional omission — "patient allergic to ACE inhibitors" is a clinically necessary note, not paperwork.
4. Assign clear ownership for reconciliation at each transition — unclear accountability leads to gaps.
5. Pharmacists performing medication reconciliation catch significantly more discrepancies than nurses alone — involve pharmacy in high-complexity patients.
6. Never leave reconciliation until after the discharge order is closed — incomplete reconciliation is a leading cause of post-discharge medication errors.
