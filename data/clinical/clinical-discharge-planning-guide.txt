# Cerner Discharge Planning Workflow Guide

## Overview

Discharge planning in Cerner Millennium is a multidisciplinary workflow coordinated across case management, social work, nursing, pharmacy, and providers. The goal is to ensure safe, timely patient transitions from the inpatient setting with appropriate follow-up, equipment, medication instructions, and community support in place. Cerner supports discharge planning through Care Management tools, Discharge PowerForms, Medication Reconciliation, and Patient Education modules.

## Discharge Planning Workflow

### Step 1: Initial Discharge Assessment (Admission)

Within 24–48 hours of admission, case management and social work complete a discharge planning assessment:
1. Identify anticipated discharge disposition (home, skilled nursing facility, long-term care, hospice, inpatient rehabilitation).
2. Assess barriers to discharge (housing instability, caregiver absence, equipment needs, insurance limitations).
3. Begin arranging post-acute resources if needed (SNF bed search, home health referral, DME orders).

In PowerChart, case managers use the Care Management PowerForm or the Care Management MPage to document:
- Estimated length of stay (ELOS)
- Anticipated discharge date (ADD)
- Discharge disposition
- Barriers to discharge
- Post-acute service needs

### Step 2: Interdisciplinary Team Communication

The multidisciplinary team meets (daily or as needed) to review discharge readiness:
- Physician/NP/PA: clinical status, medical stability criteria met?
- Nursing: patient and caregiver education complete?
- Case Management: post-acute placement confirmed?
- Social Work: psychosocial and transportation barriers resolved?
- Pharmacy: discharge medication reconciliation complete?
- Therapy (PT/OT/Speech): functional assessment and equipment needs identified?

Communication is documented via:
- Interdisciplinary Notes in the patient's chart
- Care Team MPage (shows all team members' status updates in one view)
- Length-of-Stay/Discharge Readiness flags in the patient list view

### Step 3: Discharge Order Placement

The discharging provider places a Discharge Order in CPOE:
1. Open Orders > Add Order > search "Discharge."
2. Select the appropriate discharge type:
   - **Discharge to Home** — standard home discharge
   - **Discharge to Home with Home Health Services** — home health referral required
   - **Discharge to Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF)** — requires SNF orders and placement confirmation
   - **Discharge to Inpatient Rehabilitation** — requires rehab admission order
   - **Discharge to Hospice** — triggers hospice election documentation
   - **AMA (Against Medical Advice)** — requires AMA form completion
3. Set target discharge date and time.
4. The discharge order triggers downstream workflows: medication reconciliation prompt, patient education, discharge instructions.

### Step 4: Medication Reconciliation at Discharge

The provider or clinical pharmacist completes discharge medication reconciliation:
1. Open Medication Reconciliation from the patient's chart or from the discharge workflow prompt.
2. Review the three columns:
   - Home Medications (medications listed at admission)
   - Hospital Medications (current inpatient orders)
   - Discharge Medications (what will be prescribed for home)
3. For each medication, select: Continue, Stop, New Medication, or Modified Dose.
4. Generate the discharge prescription list.
5. Send prescriptions electronically to the patient's pharmacy (SureScripts integration) or print.

### Step 5: Patient and Caregiver Education

Nursing and pharmacy provide discharge education:
1. Open the Patient Education module from the patient's chart.
2. Select appropriate education materials:
   - Diagnosis-specific education (e.g., "Heart Failure Management at Home")
   - Medication education (auto-generated from discharge medication list)
   - Activity restrictions
   - Diet instructions
   - Wound care instructions
   - Follow-up appointment reminders
3. Review education materials with the patient and caregiver.
4. Document: patient's understanding (verbalized understanding, teach-back completed), language preference, materials provided.
5. Print or email education materials to the patient.

### Step 6: Discharge Instructions

The discharge instructions document is generated from the patient's chart:
1. Open Discharge Instructions from the chart menu.
2. The system auto-populates:
   - Patient demographics and admission/discharge dates
   - Diagnosis (from the encounter's diagnosis list)
   - Discharge medications (from medication reconciliation)
   - Follow-up appointments (from the Scheduling module)
   - Activity restrictions and diet instructions
   - Return to ED precautions (when to seek emergency care)
3. Provider and nurse review and sign the instructions.
4. Patient and caregiver receive a signed copy (print or patient portal delivery).

### Step 7: Follow-Up Appointment Scheduling

Before discharge:
1. Provider places follow-up appointment orders in CPOE (e.g., "Primary Care Follow-up within 7 days," "Cardiology Follow-up within 30 days").
2. Scheduling staff or the patient's care coordinator books the appointments using Cerner Scheduling.
3. Confirmed appointments populate the discharge instructions automatically.

For 30-day readmission risk reduction:
- High-risk patients (prior admissions, complex medication regimens, no PCP) receive a post-discharge phone call within 48–72 hours (Case Management workflow).
- Transitional care programs may be triggered by readmission risk score from the Length-of-Stay MPage.

### Step 8: Physical Discharge

1. Nursing confirms all discharge criteria are met (medication reconciliation complete, instructions signed, transportation arranged).
2. IV access removed, monitoring equipment discontinued.
3. Belongings returned to patient.
4. Patient escorted to exit (wheelchair per policy).
5. Nursing documents "Patient Discharged" in PowerChart — updates the encounter status.
6. ADT system triggers a discharge transaction (A03 HL7 message) to downstream systems (billing, lab cancellations, pharmacy stop orders).

## Special Discharge Scenarios

### Against Medical Advice (AMA)
1. Patient expresses intent to leave AMA.
2. Provider documents the risks of leaving against advice.
3. Patient signs the AMA form (or refusal of the form is documented).
4. Discharge instructions still provided.
5. AMA is documented as the discharge disposition.

### Discharge to SNF
1. Case manager identifies appropriate SNF with an available bed.
2. Provider completes the skilled nursing orders (rehab, wound care, IV antibiotics, etc.).
3. The SNF clinical summary (face-to-face evaluation) is required by Medicare within 30 days.
4. Transfer documentation generated from PowerChart: transfer orders, medication list, clinical summary.

### Hospice Discharge
1. Patient and family have elected hospice care.
2. Provider documents terminal diagnosis and 6-month prognosis.
3. Hospice election form signed.
4. All curative/life-prolonging orders discontinued or transitioned to comfort-focused orders.
5. Hospice agency receives clinical summary and medication list.

## Best Practices

1. Begin discharge planning on the day of admission — late planning drives avoidable delays and excess length of stay.
2. Set and communicate the Anticipated Discharge Date (ADD) each day — align the entire care team.
3. Complete medication reconciliation at every transition point (admission, transfer, discharge).
4. Use teach-back to confirm patient understanding of discharge instructions — do not assume comprehension.
5. Schedule follow-up appointments before the patient leaves — verbal instructions to "call your doctor" result in low follow-up rates.
6. High-risk patients (heart failure, COPD, sepsis) should have care coordination follow-up within 48 hours.
