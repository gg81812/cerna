# Cerner Patient Safety Event Reporting Guide

## Overview

Patient safety event reporting in healthcare organizations using Cerner Millennium is typically handled through a dedicated Patient Safety / Incident Reporting module. Cerner's primary offering in this space is the RL6 (Risk & Compliance) module, which integrates with Millennium clinical data. Some organizations also use third-party solutions (Quantros, Quantros CAP, Origami, or RLDatix) connected via Cerner integration.

This guide covers the workflow for reporting patient safety events, near misses, and adverse events in a Cerner-integrated environment.

## Types of Reportable Events

### Adverse Events
Events that cause patient harm that are not expected outcomes of the patient's illness:
- Medication errors (wrong drug, dose, route, patient, or time)
- Patient falls (with or without injury)
- Pressure injuries acquired during hospitalization
- Healthcare-associated infections (CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI, VAP)
- Procedure-related complications
- Transfusion reactions
- Diagnostic errors with patient harm

### Near Misses (Close Calls)
Events that had the potential for harm but did not reach the patient:
- Medication intercepted before administration
- BCMA scan mismatch caught and corrected
- Incorrect patient identification caught before a procedure

### Sentinel Events
Serious, unexpected events involving death, severe physical or psychological harm, or risk thereof. Sentinel events trigger mandatory root cause analysis (RCA) and Joint Commission reporting requirements.

### Good Catches
Proactive identification and correction of a safety hazard before it causes harm. Organizations actively encourage Good Catch reporting to build a safety culture.

## Reporting Workflow

### Step 1: Recognize the Event
The clinical staff member involved in or witnessing the event initiates the report. Reports should be submitted as soon as possible after the event — ideally within the same shift.

### Step 2: Access the Safety Reporting System
From PowerChart:
1. Open the patient's chart (for patient-linked events).
2. Click the Safety Event icon in the toolbar, or navigate to: Clinical > Patient Safety > New Event Report.
3. Alternatively, access the standalone safety reporting portal (URL provided by your organization).

Some facilities have a direct shortcut on the PowerChart toolbar for faster access during busy clinical situations.

### Step 3: Complete the Event Report

A standard patient safety event report captures:

**Patient Information** (auto-populated from Millennium if accessed from the chart):
- Patient name, MRN, encounter ID
- Admission date, unit, bed
- Attending provider

**Event Details**:
- Event date and time
- Event type (from a standardized code value list)
- Event description (free text narrative of what happened)
- Contributing factors (equipment failure, communication breakdown, training gap, fatigue, staffing, etc.)
- Witnesses to the event

**Outcome**:
- Level of patient harm (None, Minor, Moderate, Major, Death — per NCC MERP or similar taxonomy)
- Immediate actions taken
- Notification of attending provider (Yes/No, and when)
- Notification of family (Yes/No)

**Medications** (for medication error reports):
- Medication name and NDC
- Ordered dose vs. administered dose
- Error type (prescribing, transcribing, dispensing, administering, monitoring)
- Detection point (pharmacist review, BCMA, nurse, patient)

### Step 4: Submit the Report
- Click Submit to send the report to the patient safety team.
- The reporter receives a confirmation number.
- The report is routed to the appropriate reviewer (Risk Management, Patient Safety Officer, unit manager) per organizational workflow rules.

### Step 5: Review and Investigation

The patient safety team:
1. Reviews the report for completeness and severity.
2. Classifies the event using the organization's taxonomy (may auto-map to NCC MERP for medications).
3. Conducts follow-up investigation (RCA for sentinel/serious events).
4. Documents corrective actions.
5. Closes the report with outcome and action plan documentation.

## Medication Error Reporting

Medication errors are among the most common safety events in Cerner-integrated environments. Key reporting categories:

### Error Types (NCC MERP Categories)
- Category A: Circumstances that could cause error but did not reach the patient
- Category B: Error occurred but did not reach the patient
- Category C: Error reached patient but caused no harm
- Category D: Error reached patient; required monitoring to confirm no harm
- Category E–I: Error reached patient and caused temporary to permanent harm or death

### Integration with BCMA Data
Cerner BCMA generates automatic event records for:
- Scan mismatches that were overridden by a nurse
- Medications administered outside the allowed time window
- Duplicate administrations attempted

These BCMA records can be linked to safety event reports for more complete investigation data.

## Fall Event Reporting

When a patient falls:
1. Immediately ensure patient safety (call for help if needed).
2. Notify the provider and document the fall assessment in the patient's chart.
3. Complete the post-fall assessment PowerForm in the patient's eMAR or nursing documentation:
   - Time and location of fall
   - Was the patient found on the floor or observed falling?
   - Injury assessment (None, Laceration, Fracture, Head injury, etc.)
   - Fall prevention measures in place at time of fall
   - Was the fall witnessed?
4. File a patient safety event report in the incident reporting system.
5. Update the Fall Risk Assessment in PowerForms.
6. Activate fall prevention care plan if not already in place.

## Pressure Injury Reporting

Hospital-acquired pressure injuries (HAPIs) are tracked as patient safety events:
1. Nursing identifies a new or worsened pressure injury during skin assessment.
2. Wound care nurse or skin champion evaluates and stages the injury.
3. Stage 2–4, unstageable, and deep tissue injuries are reportable events.
4. Complete a pressure injury safety event report.
5. Stage 3, Stage 4, and unstageable injuries are CMS-reportable "never events."

## Regulatory Reporting

### Joint Commission
Sentinel events must be reported to The Joint Commission within 72 hours. A Root Cause Analysis (RCA) must be completed and submitted within 45 days.

### CMS
Certain "never events" (Stage 3/4 pressure injuries, certain falls with injury, wrong-site surgeries) trigger mandatory CMS reporting and may affect hospital payment.

### State Health Departments
Many states require mandatory reporting of serious adverse events to the state health department within 24–72 hours.

### Integration with External Registries
Cerner integration can push anonymized event data to:
- AHRQ Patient Safety Organization (PSO) data repositories
- State patient safety reporting systems
- National Hospital Patient Safety Reporting (NHR)

## Confidentiality and Protections

Patient safety event reports filed through an approved Patient Safety Organization (PSO) program are protected under the PSQIA (Patient Safety and Quality Improvement Act) — they cannot be used in malpractice litigation. Ensure your organization's reporting system is registered with a federally-listed PSO to receive this protection.

## Best Practices

1. Report all events, near misses, and Good Catches — a strong reporting culture is the foundation of safety improvement.
2. Focus on systems, not individuals — safety events are almost always system failures, not individual failures.
3. Submit reports promptly — details are most accurate immediately after the event.
4. Never use safety event reports for punitive actions against individual staff — this chills reporting culture.
5. Close the loop — share aggregate safety data and corrective actions with frontline staff.
6. Review BCMA override data proactively — it predicts medication errors before they cause harm.
